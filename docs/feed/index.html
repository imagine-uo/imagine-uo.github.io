Seven members of our research group attended: Marina Iturrate-Bobes, Nicolás Carrizosa, David Nieto-Barba, Darío Tagarro, Ignacio Montes, Raúl Pérez-Fernández and Enrique Miranda.

The conference program consisted of 14 parallel sessions and four plenary talks. Among the plenary speakers, we had the opportunity to listen to Marco Zaffalon, a frequent collaborator of Enrique Miranda, and Ángeles Gil, our colleague at the University of Oviedo, who summarised her long career devoted to the analysis of fuzzy data.

The members of IMAGINE organized two special sessions: one on “Aggregation Theory in Non-Traditional Contexts”, comprising three presentations, and another on “Theoretical and Applied Aspects of Imprecise Probabilities”, comprising nine presentations.

At the conference, we presented seven contributions. On Monday, June 15, during the special session on aggregation theory, Marina Iturrate-Bobes and Raúl Pérez-Fernández presented the papers “Two-sample goodness-of-fit tests to a location-scale family using OWA-based skewness coefficients” and “Asymptotic confidence intervals for a skewness parameter using OWA functions”.

On Tuesday, June 16, during the special session on imprecise probabilities, Darío Tagarro, Nicolás Carrizosa, Enrique Miranda and Ignacio Montes presented four papers: “An order-theoretic study of the credal sets generated by comparative probabilities”, “Decision making with generalized distortion models”, “Weighted Total Variation distances for contextual robustification”, and “On the commutativity of the aggregation of capacities and the natural extension to gambles”.

Finally, on Wednesday, June 17, David Nieto-Barba presented his paper “On the specific commutativity with Bayesian conditioning of systematic aggregation rules for probability measures”.

On Thursday, June 18, we enjoyed a guided tour through the city center of Rome, visiting iconic places such as the Trevi Fountain and the Pantheon. Afterwards, we attended a wonderful gala dinner. During the dinner, the traditional awards were given, and Nicolás Carrizosa received the IJAR Best Student Paper Award.

Overall, during these days we enjoyed both the scientific and social aspects of the conference, and we will keep many fond memories of it. Last but not least, we would like to congratulate the organising committee for all the work they put into making this conference such a success. See you in Warsaw at the IPMU 2028 conference!

]]> New published paper at the International Journal of General Systems! http://imagine-wp.local/2026/06/15/new-published-paper-at-the-international-journal-of-general-systems/ Mon, 15 Jun 2026 12:47:00 +0000 http://imagine-wp.local/?p=1158 We are pleased to announce that our paper

On the closedness of imprecise probability models under aggregation

has been accepted for publication in the International Journal of General Systems.

This paper, authored by Enrique Miranda and Ignacio Montes, explores the aggregation of particular families of imprecise probability models, and continues our line of research on the aggregation of imprecise probability models, initiated with our previous publication “A Comparative Analysis of Aggregation Rules for Coherent Lower Previsions” and the conference paper “On the commutativity of the aggregation of coherent lower probabilities and the natural extension to gambles”.

In particular, in this contribution we consider six different models, namely comparative probabilities (Section 4), 2-monotone capacities (Section 5), probability intervals (Section 6), belief functions (Section 7), minitive measures (Section 8), and p-boxes (Section 9). For each of these models, we analyse whether several aggregation rules (conjunction, disjunction, convex mixtures, Pareto, conjunction–disjunction, and maximal consistent subsets rules) are closed within the family, in the sense that if the initial models to be aggregated belong to one of these families, the resulting model also belongs to the same family.

]]> New published paper at Fuzzy Sets and Systems! http://imagine-wp.local/2026/04/10/new-published-paper-at-fuzzy-sets-and-systems/ Fri, 10 Apr 2026 12:52:37 +0000 http://imagine-wp.local/?p=1162 We are happy to announce that our paper

On the use of OWA functions for robustifying the Lilliefors test of goodness-of-fit to a location-scale family

has been accepted for publication in Fuzzy Sets and Systems.

The aim of this paper, authored by Marina Iturrate-Bobes, Raúl Pérez-Fernández and Bernard de Baets, is to construct a robust version of the Lilliefors normality test that is not affected by outliers.

To this end, Ordered Weighted Averaging (OWA) functions are used to bound the range in which the statistic is computed, as well as to provide robust estimates of the mean and standard deviation. Moreover, this robust version can be used to test goodness-of-fit to any distribution, not only the normal distribution. This is explored in Section 3, where an explicit formula for the statistic is provided. Section 4 presents an experimental study in which the role of each parameter is analysed and the potential of the test in the presence of outliers is demonstrated.

]]> New published paper at Statistical Papers! http://imagine-wp.local/2026/03/13/new-published-paper-at-statistical-papers/ Fri, 13 Mar 2026 09:46:23 +0000 http://imagine-wp.local/?p=1059 We are pleased to announce that our paper

Confidence intervals with imprecise data

has been accepted for publication in Statistical Papers.

This paper, written by Darío Tagarro, Raúl Pérez-Fernández and Enrique Miranda, addresses the problem of accounting for imprecision in the process of measuring a continuous variable in the context of confidence intervals for parameters of probability distributions.  We generalize the classical notion of confidence interval in the presence of imprecision by introducing the notions of inner and outer confidence interval, studying different properties of both mathematical constructs and providing their explicit expressions in five prominent cases in the field of Statistics.

After introducing in Section 2 some basic concepts on random sets, random intervals and confidence intervals, we give our definition of the inner and outer confidence intervals in Section 3. In Section 4 we present some properties related to their coverage and characterization. In particular, we derive an explicit expression of both constructs in terms of the extrema of the lower and upper bound functions of the confidence interval. Finally, we discuss in Section 5 five relevant examples of confidence intervals and provide the explicit formulae of the inner and outer confidence intervals under imprecision. For this aim, we have introduced the notions of quasivertex and 2-quasivertex, that have allowed us to simplify the optimization problem associated with the computation of the inner and outer intervals.

]]> New published paper at FSS! http://imagine-wp.local/2026/02/16/new-published-paper-at-fss/ Mon, 16 Feb 2026 15:11:46 +0000 http://imagine-wp.local/?p=1033 We are pleased to announce that our paper:

Neighbourhood models induced by the Euclidean distance and the Kullback-Leibler divergence


has been accepted for publication in Fuzzy Sets and Systems.

This paper, authored by Ignacio Montes, continues the research on distortion or neighbourhood models, a class of robust probabilistic models obtained by creating a neighbourhood of probability measures centred on a given initial probability, with a given radius and with respect to a distorting function used to compare probability measures. In particular, this paper investigates the imprecise probability models that arise when considering the Euclidean distance or the Kullback–Leibler divergence as the distorting function.

To this end, after introducing some preliminaries in Section 2, Section 3 is devoted to the distortion model determined by the Euclidean distance. A closed formula for the lower prevision associated with this model is obtained, and some interesting properties are presented. For example, while the lower prevision is not 2-monotone on gambles, it is 2-monotone on events.

Section 4 performs a similar study for the distortion model induced by the Kullback–Leibler divergence. Not surprisingly, this model is more difficult to handle from an analytical point of view. Nevertheless, it is possible to prove that, like the Euclidean model, it is 2-monotone on events but not on gambles.

Section 5 presents a comparison of the Euclidean and Kullback–Leibler models with some well-known distortion models, such as the linear vacuous model (also known as epsilon-contamination), the pari-mutuel model, the total variation model, and the constant odds ratio model. This comparative study shows that, at least from a practical point of view, the Euclidean and Kullback–Leibler models do not possess particularly attractive properties, even though their utility and interpretation as distortion models are unquestionable.

]]> SPMS 2026: Special Session on Theoretical and applied aspects of imprecise probabilities http://imagine-wp.local/2025/12/23/spms-2026-special-session-on-theoretical-and-applied-aspects-of-imprecise-probabilities/ Tue, 23 Dec 2025 08:00:02 +0000 http://imagine-wp.local/?p=1000 The SPMS 2026 conference:

International Conference on Soft Methods in Probability and Statistics 2026

will take place from September 15 to 18, 2026, in Lecce, Italy. The practical information about the conference can be found on the SPMS 2026 website.

Enrique Miranda and Raúl Pérez-Fernández are organising a special session about imprecise probabilities:

Theoretical and applied aspects of imprecise probabilities

This session is devoted to Imprecise Probability Theory. This theory involves all the mathematical models that can be used as more flexible tools than usual Probability Theory when the available information is scarce, vague or incomplete. It includes lower previsions, n-monotone capacities, belief functions, possibility measures, or non-additive measures, among others.

This special session aims to include papers related to Imprecise Probabilities that either present a significant advance in the foundations or show potential applications in real problems. In addition, papers where the connection between imprecise probability theory and other fields such as fuzzy sets or game theory is emphasised are also welcome.
